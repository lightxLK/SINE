# Comprehensive Literature Review: Agentic AI & Sentiment Intelligence (2021–2026)

This literature review maps current academic and industry advancements against the **Agentic Sentiment Intelligence System** developed by Lokesh Patra. It highlights how this project specifically addresses critical "Gaps" in contemporary NLP and Agentic research.

## Literature Survey

| Author(s) & Year | Research Paper Title | Key Contributions | Literature Gap | Project Fix (Our Solution) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maragheh et al. (2025)** | ARAG: Agentic Retrieval Augmented Generation for Personalized Recommendation | Integrates multi-agent collaboration (NLI, Item Ranker) into RAG pipelines | Fails to capture nuanced user preferences in dynamic scenarios due to static retrieval heuristics | Employs a Multi-Tiered Agentic Architecture that combines neural normalization with contextual specialists to decode complex intent |
| **Cao & Hu (2026)** | A Solicit-Then-Suggest Model of Agentic Purchasing | Develops multi-round conversations to refine user preference models in preference space | Traditional e-commerce lacks the ability to learn preferences from messy, unstructured descriptions | Uses a Linguistic Quality Node (LQN) to programmatically "repair" unstructured descriptions before analysis |
| **Ayanami0730 (2026)** | A-RAG: Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Hierarchical Retrieval Interfaces | Exposes hierarchical tools (keyword, semantic, chunk) for agentic autonomy | Existing RAG systems are too rigid and do not allow models to participate in retrieval decisions | Implements an intelligence pipeline (Normalize → Analyze → Explain) where agents autonomously refine input quality |
| **Scott et al. (2025)** | Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis on Amazon Product Reviews Using a Novel Hybrid ML Algorithm | Introduces SVHA using Random Forest and SVM to improve classification | Standard rule-based extraction struggles with complex language structures in long reviews | Applies Sublinear TF-IDF Tuning and neural normalization to simplify complex text for better feature weighting |
| **Wen & Rezapour (2026)** | A Transformer and Prototype-based Interpretable Model for Contextual Sarcasm Detection | Uses prototype tensors for sentence-level XAI by referencing similar training examples | Word-level XAI (SHAP/LIME) is non-intuitive for sarcasm/analogies, lacking clear intent explanations | The Reasoning Engine (RE) provides symbolic "Conflict Logic" to explain exactly why a sarcastic remark was flagged |
| **Daraghmi & Zyadeh (2026)** | Sentiment Classification of Amazon Product Reviews: A Comparative Study | Benchmarks ML and DL models (RoBERTa, LSTM, CNN) under unified conditions | Traditional models fail to capture sarcasm and negation reversals (e.g., "amazing... it broke") | Fine-tuned DistilRoBERTa Specialist uses "Contextual Persistence" to correctly classify "Perfectly mis-crafted" as Negative |
| **Thogesan et al. (2025)** | Integration of Explainable AI Techniques with Large Language Models for Sentiment Analysis | Applies layer-wise SHAP analysis to BERT for granular token attribution | LLMs frequently function as "black boxes," offering no rationale for forecasts in critical apps | Incorporates a symbolic inference layer (RE) that maps neural confidence to human-readable business insights |
| **Dubey et al. (2025)** | Unpacking Sarcasm: A Contextual and Transformer-Based Approach for Improved Detection | Combines context summarization with metadata-enhanced embeddings in RoBERTa | Lack of structured preprocessing leads to high resource usage and suboptimal real-time performance | Optimized for CPU-bound environments, achieving a latency reduction from 16s to 1.1s through beam search pruning |
| **Akter et al. (2025)** | Sentiment Analysis of Consumer Feedback and Its Impact on Business Strategies | Validates that BERT outperforms ML (94.2% accuracy) for business intelligence | High computational resource demands of BERT limit accessibility for SMEs without expensive GPUs | Implements Hardware Democratization by optimizing DistilRoBERTa for consumer-grade (8GB RAM/CPU) devices |
| **Agrawal (2025)** | The Future of E-Commerce with Agentic AI: Personalized, Task-Oriented AI | Proposes specialized agents for life domains (parenting, meal planning) | Fragmented AI implementations lack a goal-oriented understanding across the shopping journey | Treats sentiment as an integrated intelligence pipeline, moving beyond isolated classification to end-to-end reasoning |
| **Trivedi (2025)** | Explainable Agentic AI: Transforming E-Commerce Search With Transparency | Postulates conceptual models (Goal Engine, Decision Core) for agentic search | Shortcomings in transparency of autonomous implementations hinder user trust | Develops an "Explanation Layer" (RE) that provides a verifiable reasoning trace for every sentiment score |
| **Chauhan & Kumar (2024)** | A Transformer-based Model for Detecting Multilingual Sarcasm in Social Media Posts | Uses mBERT to detect sarcasm in code-mixed (Tamil/Malayalam) text | Emojis and informal slang degrade accuracy in multilingual social media settings | Employs Emoji Demojization and a custom slang map (e.g., 'gr8' → 'great') to preserve emotive tone |
| **Kaseb & Farouk (2023)** | SAIDS: A Novel Approach for Sentiment Analysis Informed of Dialect and Sarcasm | Introduces "informed decision" architecture where sarcasm outputs inform sentiment models | Identifying sentiment in sarcastic text is a primary hurdle because literal and intended meanings conflict | Developed an Active Mining Workflow to extract "Ironic Dissonance" cases, teaching the model the "truth" behind the sarcasm |
| **Abu Farha et al. (2022)** | Sarcasm Detection is Way Too Easy! An Empirical Comparison of Human and Machine | Benchmarks human vs. machine performance on first-party annotated sets | Machine models struggle with context-dependent sarcasm that relies on "world knowledge" | Uses a Specialist Engine trained on 336 "Hard Cases" to prioritize contextual "dealbreakers" over surface praise |
| **Romio, G. (2026)** | 15 Best AI Sentiment Analysis Tools & Use Cases in 2026 | Reviews top-tier conversation intelligence platforms for 2026 | Basic "keyword matching" models generate "false alarms" by taking text literally | The RE flags "Conflict of Signals" to prevent false alarms, explaining when a "Dealbreaker" attribute outweighs "Incentive" words |
